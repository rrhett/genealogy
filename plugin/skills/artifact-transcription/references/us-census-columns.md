# U.S. Federal Population Census Reference (1790–1950)

Use this reference when transcribing U.S. Federal Census scans to calculate implied birth years accurately from official **Census Day** and to interpret decade-specific columns and abbreviations.

> **Age Rule:** Enumerators were legally instructed to record each person's age *as of official Census Day* (not the actual visit date), though enumerators occasionally used the visit date anyway. Always record both the **Census Day** and the **actual enumeration date** written at the top of the sheet.

---

## Census Day & Milestone Summary (1790–1950)

| Year | Official Census Day | Coverage & Survival Notes | Major Genealogical Columns Introduced / Notable Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1790** | 2 Aug 1790 | ~1/3 lost (DE, GA, KY, NJ, TN, VA schedules largely destroyed; some reconstructed from state tax lists) | Head of household name only; free white males 16+, free white males <16, free white females, all other free persons, enslaved persons (counts only). |
| **1800** | 4 Aug 1800 | Partial losses (GA, KY, NJ, TN, VA, Indiana/Mississippi Territories largely lost) | Head of household name; expanded 5 age brackets for free white males & females (<10, 10–15, 16–25, 26–44, 45+). |
| **1810** | 6 Aug 1810 | Partial losses (DC, GA, NJ, TN [except Rutherford Co.], parts of VA/IL/MI/OH) | Same age brackets as 1800; added early manufacturing data in some areas. |
| **1820** | 7 Aug 1820 | Partial losses (AL, AR, MO, NJ, parts of TN/GA/IN) | Added male 16–18 overlap column (for militia), foreign-born not naturalized, engagement in agriculture/commerce/manufactures, age brackets for free colored & enslaved persons. |
| **1830** | 1 Jun 1830 | Mostly intact | Census Day shifts to **June 1**. Narrower 5-year (<20) and 10-year (20–100+) age brackets for free white males & females; deaf/dumb/blind flags. |
| **1840** | 1 Jun 1840 | Mostly intact | Same age brackets as 1830; **Revolutionary War / military pensioners** named with exact ages on reverse/separate column (vital lead for 1750s–1760s births); literacy & school attendance counts. |
| **1850** | 1 Jun 1850 | Intact (first "modern" census) | **Every free household member named** with exact age, sex, color, occupation (males 15+), real estate value, **state/territory/country of birth**, married within the year, attended school, adult illiteracy, deaf/dumb/blind/insane/pauper/convict. *No explicit relationship to head.* Separate **Slave Schedules** (usually unnamed, age/sex/color under enslaver's name) and **Mortality Schedules** (deaths in prior 12 months). |
| **1860** | 1 Jun 1860 | Intact | Same as 1850 plus **personal estate value** alongside real estate value; separate Slave & Mortality Schedules. |
| **1870** | 1 Jun 1870 | Intact (first census naming formerly enslaved people) | Adds **father of foreign birth** and **mother of foreign birth** checkboxes, **month of birth** if born within census year, **month of marriage** if married within census year, male citizens 21+ and voting denial flags. *Still no explicit relationship to head.* |
| **1880** | 1 Jun 1880 | Intact | **Relationship to head of household** introduced; marital status (`S`, `M`, `W`/`D`); **birthplace of father and mother**; months unemployed; sick/disabled on Census Day. Soundex exists only for households with a child age 10 or under. |
| **1890** | 2 Jun 1890 | **>99% destroyed** (Jan 1921 fire at Dept. of Commerce + 1933 disposal) | Surviving population fragments (~6,160 names) exist only for small areas of AL, DC, GA, IL, MN, NJ, NY, NC, OH, SD, TX. **1890 Special Schedule of Union Veterans & Widows** survives for states **Kentucky through Wyoming** (plus parts of DC and limited earlier remnants). Use 1885/1895 state censuses, city directories, and tax lists as substitutes. |
| **1900** | 1 Jun 1900 | Intact | **Month and year of birth** (e.g., `May 1886`), years of current marriage, **mother of how many children / how many living** (essential for locating missing children who died young), **year of immigration**, years in U.S., citizenship (`Al`, `Pa`, `Na`), home owned/rented (`O`/`R`, `F`/`M`) and farm schedule number. |
| **1910** | 15 Apr 1910 | Intact | **Marriage number (`M1`, `M2`, etc.)** and years of current marriage; children born / living (last census to ask all women); **mother tongue** (for foreign-born & parents); **Union/Confederate veteran (`UA`, `UN`, `CA`, `CN`)**; industry & class of worker (`W`, `OA`, `Emp`). |
| **1920** | 1 Jan 1920 | Intact | Census Day moved to **January 1** (expect apparent 9-year age jumps from 1910). **Year of naturalization** added alongside year of immigration; mother tongue of person and parents. *Drops marriage duration and children born/living.* |
| **1930** | 1 Apr 1930 | Intact | **Age at first marriage** (vital for separating first vs. later marriages); **radio set (`R`)**; home value or monthly rent; military veteran flag & war (`WW`, `Sp`, `Civ`, `Phil`, `Box`, `Mex`); non-farm vs. farm indicator; unemployment schedule link. |
| **1940** | 1 Apr 1940 | Intact | **Informant marked with a circled `x` (`⊗`)** (determines primary vs. secondary information!); **residence on 1 April 1935** (city, county, state); highest grade of school completed; weeks worked & wage/salary income in 1939; **Supplemental Questions (5% sample — lines 14 & 29)** giving parent birthplaces, mother tongue, veteran status, Social Security deductions, and for ever-married women: married more than once, age at first marriage, children ever born. |
| **1950** | 1 Apr 1950 | Intact (released 1 Apr 2022) | Residence **1 year ago (1949)**; hours worked last week; class of worker (`P`, `G`, `O`, `NP`); **Sample Lines (20% sample — 6 lines per 30-line sheet)** with 1949 residence, parent birthplaces, education, income, veteran status (`WWII`, `WWI`, `Other`); **3.3% extra sample (last sample line on sheet)** adds prior occupation, marital duration/times married, and children ever born. |

## Common Census Abbreviations & Codes

### Marital Status
- `S` — Single
- `M` — Married (`M1` = first marriage, `M2` = second marriage in 1910)
- `Wd` / `W` — Widowed
- `D` — Divorced
- `Sep` — Separated (1950)

### Citizenship & Immigration (1900–1950)
- `Al` — Alien (not yet filed naturalization papers)
- `Pa` — First papers filed (Declaration of Intention)
- `Na` — Naturalized citizen
- `Am Cit` — American citizen born abroad (1920–1940)

### Home Ownership & Tenure
- `O` / `R` — Owned / Rented
- `F` / `M` — Owned Free of mortgage / Mortgaged
- `F` / `H` — Farm / House

### Employment Class (1910–1950)
- `W` / `PW` / `P` — Wage or salary worker (private)
- `GW` / `G` — Government worker
- `Emp` / `E` — Employer
- `OA` / `O` — Working on own account (self-employed)
- `NP` — Unpaid family worker

### Racial / Color Terms (Transcribe Verbatim)
Historical censuses used period-specific designations (`W`, `B`, `M`/`Mu`, `In`/`Ind`, `Ch`, `Jp`, `Mex` in 1930, `Neg` in 1930–1950). Always transcribe the exact code written on the schedule without modernizing or altering the original text.

## Pitfalls to Watch During Transcription

1. **Relationship to Head Before 1880:** Never assume a household member in 1850–1870 is a spouse or biological child solely from order and surname—they may be a sibling, parent, niece/nephew, stepchild, or in-law. Record relationships from 1850–1870 alone as `probable` or `speculative` unless corroborated.
2. **Age Heaping & Shift:** Ages before 1900 frequently round to multiples of 5 or 0. Always check Census Day (especially 1910 April 15 vs. 1920 January 1) before flagging a 1-year birth discrepancy.
3. **Informant Reliability:** Before 1940, the informant is usually unrecorded (`information_type: undetermined`). In 1940, check who bears the circled `x` (`⊗`): if a neighbor or young child gave the answers, birthplaces and ages for adults are `secondary` or `mixed`.
4. **Infants Under Age 1:** Fractions like `5/12` mean 5 months old as of Census Day (born ~5 months prior); in 1950, infants born in Jan–Mar 1950 were recorded with their birth month (`Jan`, `Feb`, `Mar`) instead of a fraction.
5. **Non-Population Schedules:** Remember to check companion schedules when available—**Agriculture** (1850–1880), **Mortality** (1850–1880), **Slave** (1850–1860), **Industry/Manufacturing** (1850–1880), and **DDD (Defective, Dependent, Delinquent)** (1880).
